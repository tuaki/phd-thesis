The work presented in this thesis summarizes the results of author's doctoral research in the field of multi-model database management. It extends an existing approach to modeling, transformations, and querying multi-model data with novel methods for evolution management and self-adaptation.

The main part of the thesis is a collection of ten papers (\paperRef{vldb_2024} to \paperRef{vldb_2025_dortdb}) published in peer-reviewed conferences and journals in the years 2022--2026. A full \hyperref[chap:publications]{list of the author's publications} is included at the end of the thesis.

This collection is preceded by a commentary that provides a high-level overview of the research and its contributions. First, it formulates the vision and the overall research goals. Then it describes the existing approach for modeling, transformations, and querying multi-model data that we use as a foundation for our research. Next, it explains the two main research directions---evolution management and self-adaptation. For both of them, it defines the open problems, presents the proposed solutions, and discusses the contributions of the individual papers. Finally, it discusses two related concepts to our research---benchmarking and an alternative approach to multi-model data querying.
