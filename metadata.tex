% Abstract (recommended length around 80-200 words; this is not a copy of your thesis assignment!)
\def\Abstract{%
Multi-model databases combine relational, document, graph, and other data models, but their management remains fragmented across incompatible schemas, query languages, and adaptation mechanisms. This thesis develops a category-theoretic foundation for representing conceptual schemas, model-specific logical representations, and the mappings between them in a unified way. On top of this foundation, it defines a model-independent approach to querying, a systematic method for propagating schema changes to logical models, data, and queries, and a self-adaptation approach that searches for better logical configurations under changing workloads. The adaptation process combines workload-driven search with learned performance estimation, allowing candidate configurations to be compared before full materialization. The thesis contributes formal models, propagation and synchronization algorithms, and an initial experimental validation. Together, the results show that multi-model data management can be described coherently, automated to a substantial degree, and adapted to evolving application needs.
}
